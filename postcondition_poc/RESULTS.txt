Concrete observed failure: HumanEval/8, seed 47.
Both matrix-based responses say in their rationale that candidate 0 is invalid,
yet their validity string SCCCCCSSSS marks candidate 0 as supported. The shared
selector faithfully chooses that candidate from the machine-readable fields.
Its predicate checks a product of only numbers[0] and numbers[1]. On the
archived canonical input [3,5,7], the required product is 105; the predicate demands
15 and rejects the correct program. Direct selection chose candidates 1,4,8.
This exposes a mismatch between prose reasoning and the compact machine-readable
assessment. Repairing that interface would require another fair comparison;
the repair alone would not establish the proposed method's benefit.

Other observed false alarms in the same checkpoint include an empty parentheses
string rejected by len(output)==len(input.split(' ')), and binary conversion
with int('',2) on permitted empty strings. These outcomes were preserved,
not repaired or removed from the evaluated packages.

Contract-boundary qualification: HumanEval/1 has the input '()()(()'. The supplied
executable domain prevents negative prefix balance but does not require final
balance zero. The canonical implementation discards the unmatched suffix. A
postcondition demanding reconstruction of the complete input therefore fails
this finite oracle, although the prose can be read as assuming balanced groups.
We do not claim this post is unambiguously wrong under every reading of the
natural-language contract. Its original score remains preserved.
