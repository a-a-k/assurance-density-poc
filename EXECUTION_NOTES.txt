Execution notes, 23 October 2026

The finite 200-row policy, mutation operators, exact minimum-size objective,
secondary case-ID objective, and post-selection historical evaluation were
frozen in inputs/protocol.json before measuring Requests.

Two instrumentation/interpretation errors were found during development:

1. The initial branch matrix intersected raw coverage.py arcs with normalized
   JSON-report arcs. Multi-line statements were therefore undercounted. The
   corrected implementation applies coverage.py's own translation and asserts
   that the union of all per-case branches equals the complete-domain report.
   The dataset and objectives are unchanged. Initial summary results were not
   validated and are not used in the completed PoC report.

2. A failure anywhere in Requests 2.19.1 is not necessarily a reproduction of
   CVE-2018-18074. The complete policy also identifies port-change and unusual
   upgrade discrepancies. Named CVE detection is therefore evaluated only on
   the HTTPS_DOWNGRADE category. Named 2.20.0 regression detection uses only
   DEFAULT_PORT_EQUIVALENCE. All other discrepancies are reported separately.
   This corrects the outcome definition; historical outcomes never enter the
   minimum-size or case-ID selection objectives.

This is a retrospective, analyst-constructed pilot. Freezing inputs does not
make the known historical defects prospective or blind. It is not an LLM test
generation experiment. No maintainer reviewed or approved the finite policy.

Exploratory follow-up, explicitly separate from the frozen primary analysis:
diagnose.py isolates same-host HTTPS-to-HTTP credential retention within the
fixed implementation, while leaving other finite-domain observations correct.
It also constructs a minimum-size structural reduction constrained to contain
a downgrade observation. These diagnostic queries were added after the initial
results. They do not modify the frozen baseline selections or their objectives,
and do not constitute prospective evidence of improved defect detection.

The exploratory follow-up was then generalized to all seven frozen categories
in audit_ambiguity.py. For each category, two fixed-optimum feasibility queries
look for reductions containing and omitting an assigned observation. This
audit reads neither historical outcomes nor isolated-fault outcomes. Its
development was informed by the primary results; it is not represented as a
prospectively evaluated or uniquely novel optimization technique.
